# Deen Buddy

**Stay on track. Together.**

A social accountability app for Muslims. Every prayer app tells you *when* Maghrib
is — almost nobody misses a prayer because they didn't know the time. They miss it
because nobody was waiting.

Deen Buddy is built around the smallest unit of accountability there is: one person
who notices.

1. Download the `.apk` on your phone.
2. Open it from your notifications or Files app.
3. Android will ask permission to install from this source — allow it.
4. Open Deen Buddy.

This is a test build, signed with a development key. Android will warn you that it
came from outside the Play Store; that's expected.

## What it does

**The five prayers, with a witness.** Log each prayer inside its own window — the app
won't let you tick things off later, which is what makes every other number in it
mean something.

**Circles.** One person, or a few. They see your day, you see theirs. Nudge someone
whose window is closing, or send a du'a — which earns nothing at all, on purpose.

**Streaks that understand life.** Each prayer has its own streak, plus a Golden
Streak for all five. Exempt days pause rather than break. Every ten Golden Days earns
a *Rukhsah* — a concession you can spend to hold the counter after a bad day.

**Stages.** A habit ladder from "one prayer a day" through congregation, making up
missed prayers, and Sunnah practice.

**Missions.** Good deeds to take on alone or with your Circle.

---

## Two things it deliberately refuses

**No leaderboards.** Nothing in this app ranks people against each other. Ranking
worship invites showing off and judgement, and family groups become surveillance.
Groups show an aggregate, never an ordering.

**No streak-loss threats.** "Your 40-day streak is about to end" is the
highest-converting notification in consumer apps, and it is banned here. Using fear
of losing a number to drive an act of worship inverts the whole point.

There's also a **Hide XP** switch that removes the entire points system while leaving
streaks and Circles intact — because a product that claims gamification is secondary
while making it un-removable isn't telling the truth.

## On the religious content

Every Qur'anic reference and hadith in the app is registered with its source, and
**every hadith is narrated in Sahih al-Bukhari and/or Sahih Muslim.** References have
been checked; a framing review by a qualified scholar is still outstanding before any
public launch.

Where scholars genuinely differ — most importantly on making up deliberately missed
prayers — the app presents the positions with attribution and asks you to choose. It
does not pick a madhhab for you.

XP, levels and streaks are **application mechanics**. They measure behaviour the app
can see. They are not a measure of worship, sincerity, or reward.
